Privacy notice

This notice explains what the Learner Attendance & Parent Monitoring System records about learners and their guardians, why, who can see it, and what rights you have. It is written to align with the Kenyan Data Protection Act, 2019.

⚠ This is a template, not legal advice. The school adopting the system is the data controller and should have this notice reviewed against its own practice and by a qualified adviser before publication. Bracketed items must be completed by the school.

Who is responsible for this information

[School name] is the data controller for learner and guardian information held in this system. The school decides what is collected and why. Any technology supplier operates as a data processor under a written agreement and acts only on the school’s instructions.

Data protection contact: [name, role, email, telephone].

What information is collected

About learners

  • Admission number, full name, date of birth
  • Class and stream
  • Emergency contact information
  • Daily attendance status, and arrival time where the school records it
  • Confirmations that the learner left home and arrived home, with the time and any comment the guardian adds
  • Behaviour observations recorded by teachers, including positive observations
  • Comments exchanged between the school and the learner’s guardians
  • A photograph only where the school has established that it is lawful and necessary. It is not required for the system to work.

About guardians and staff

  • Name, telephone number and email address
  • The relationship to each linked learner
  • Account role and status, and sign-in records
  • For staff: staff number and class or subject assignments

The system is designed around data minimisation: it does not collect information that is not needed for the purposes below, and fields the school does not use can be switched off.

Why it is collected

  • To record attendance, which the school is required to keep.
  • To safeguard learners — in particular, to notice when a learner left home but did not arrive at school.
  • To communicate with guardians about absence, lateness, behaviour and school announcements.
  • To support learners whose attendance or behaviour suggests they need help.
  • To report to school management and, where required, to the relevant authorities.

The lawful bases relied on are typically the school’s legal obligation to maintain attendance records, the legitimate interest in safeguarding learners, and consent where an optional feature such as a photograph is used. The school should record which basis applies to each purpose.

Who can see it

Access is restricted by role and enforced in the system’s data layer, not only in what the screen displays.

  • Teachers see only learners in the classes they are assigned to.
  • Guardians see only the learners linked to them by a link the school has authorised, and never another guardian’s details.
  • Administrators see school-wide information as their role requires, and their actions are recorded in the audit log.
  • Behaviour notes marked internal to the school are not shown to guardians.

Every access and change to a record is logged with the account responsible and the time.

Sharing outside the school

Learner information is not sold, and is not used for advertising or profiling. It may be shared only:

  • with the guardians authorised for that learner;
  • with the Ministry of Education or another authority where the law requires it;
  • with emergency or child-protection services where a learner’s safety requires it;
  • with the school’s technology provider strictly to operate and support the system, under a written processing agreement.

Where information is stored or processed outside Kenya, the school must satisfy itself that the transfer meets the requirements of the Data Protection Act, 2019.

How long it is kept

  • Attendance and movement records: [retention period] after the end of the academic year.
  • Behaviour records and comments: [retention period].
  • Audit logs: [retention period], kept longer than the records they describe.
  • Account details: for as long as the person is connected to the school, then deleted or anonymised.

At the end of the retention period, records are deleted or irreversibly anonymised.

How it is protected

  • Role-based access control, applied at the database and application levels.
  • Individual accounts with a strong password policy; passwords are stored only as salted hashes.
  • Optional two-factor authentication.
  • Encryption in transit, with sensitive data protected at rest.
  • An append-only audit trail of record creation, editing and deletion.
  • Regular, tested backups and a documented recovery procedure.
  • Least-privilege access for staff and administrators, reviewed when roles change.
  • A procedure for assessing and, where required, reporting a personal-data breach.

Your rights

Under the Data Protection Act, 2019 you may:

  • ask what information the school holds about you or your child, and receive a copy;
  • ask for inaccurate information to be corrected;
  • ask for information to be deleted where the school is not required to keep it;
  • object to a particular use, or ask that it be restricted while a concern is resolved;
  • withdraw consent for anything that relies on consent, such as a photograph;
  • complain to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner.

Requests should go to the school office, which will respond within the period the Act allows.

Children’s data

Learner records concern children, and the Act requires particular care. Information is collected only for the purposes above, kept to the minimum required, and made visible only to those who need it. Where consent is the basis for an optional feature, it is sought from the parent or guardian, and it can be withdrawn at any time without affecting the learner’s attendance record.

Contact and complaints

To exercise any right, ask a question, or raise a concern, contact [name, role] at [email] or [telephone].

If you are not satisfied with the school’s response, you may complain to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner of Kenya.
